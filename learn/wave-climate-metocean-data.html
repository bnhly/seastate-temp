Wave climate and metocean data: where to get it free

A wave climate is the long term picture of the sea at a place: how big the waves usually are, how that changes through the year, where they come from, and how big the rare storms get. It is built from wave statistics over many years, not from a forecast.

What a wave climate covers

The usual set is the average significant wave height by month, the share of time it is above working limits (exceedance), wave period and direction, swell and wind sea, wind, weather windows and return values such as the 100-year wave height. Planning, tendering and vessel selection mostly need the monthly figures. Design needs the extremes.

Where the numbers come from

Measured data comes from wave buoys and offshore platforms. It is the best record where it exists, but buoys are few, mostly near the coast, and often have gaps.

Hindcasts rerun a wave model over past weather, giving a complete record every few hours on a grid that covers the oceans. ERA5 from ECMWF, through the Copernicus Climate Change Service, and WAVERYS, through the Copernicus Marine Service, are both global and free to download once you register. Many countries also publish their own regional hindcasts and buoy records. A hindcast is consistent and covers every point, but it smooths the peaks of the most intense storms.

Getting wave statistics without processing the data

Raw hindcast files are large and need some coding to turn into statistics. Sea State Explorer has done that for you: click any point at sea and read wave height exceedance by month, average wave height, wave period and direction, swell, wind, weather windows and the 1 to 100-year wave heights, from WAVERYS wave reanalysis (Copernicus Marine) and ERA5 wind, 1980-2023, 3 hourly. There is a one page PDF summary for each point. Named offshore fields and passages have their own pages, grouped by region. For example, the Thylacine (Otway) page gives an average significant wave height of 3.0 m over the year, with seas above 2 m about 85% of the time.

Limits

These are statistics for planning and tendering. Each figure comes from the nearest grid cell of the model, so it misses local effects close to the coast and in shallow water. Design and contract weather criteria need a project specific metocean study, prepared and checked by a specialist.
